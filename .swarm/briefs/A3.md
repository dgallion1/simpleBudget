### Why this task exists

Today an internal transfer is **dropped** at load if its description matches a
substring pattern. Two failure modes follow. A transfer whose description
misses the patterns is counted twice — the debit inflates expenses and the
credit is usually classified Income, corrupting savings rate in both
directions. And a transfer that IS matched vanishes entirely, so the user
cannot see money they actually moved.

A3 replaces dropping with **classifying**, and makes pairing — not string
matching — the thing that determines truth.

### Behavior

**1. Auto-pair.** A candidate pair is: opposite-sign rows, equal amount in
cents, **different** `AccountID`s, dates within **±4 days**, neither leg
already paired, and **at least one leg matches** `InternalTransferPatterns` or
an `IsInternalTransfer` Major Expense. A unique candidate pairs: both legs get
`TransactionType: Transfer`, `TransferClass: "paired"`, and a shared
`TransferPairKey`. Multiple candidates → closest date wins; an exact tie goes
to the review queue instead.

**2. Suspected → review queue.** Cross-account opposite-sign amount matches
inside the window with **no** pattern hit, or ambiguous ties, are *suggested*
and never auto-paired — coincidentally equal amounts are common and
auto-pairing them would silently erase real income or spending. Surface them
via `SuspectedTransfers()`. `ResolveTransfer` persists the user's decision to
`transfer_decisions.json`; a confirmed pair becomes paired on the next load, a
rejected pair is never suggested again.

**3. External leg.** A row matching the patterns (or an `IsInternalTransfer`
Major Expense) with no counterparty in the loaded data — Vanguard, Coinbase,
a brokerage whose CSV is not loaded — becomes `TransactionType: Transfer`,
`TransferClass: "external"`, and carries **no** pair key.

**4. Transfers stay in the ledger.** Replace `filterInternalTransfers` with
your classification stage. Classified rows must remain present and visible;
only their exclusion from income/expense changes. `FilteredTransfers()` keeps
returning a count for the existing UI, now counting classified transfers
rather than dropped rows.

**5. Income/outflow classification skips Transfer rows.** A row already typed
`Transfer` must not then be re-typed Income or Outflow.

**6. The consumer must actually change.** This is the requirement most likely
to be half-done, and the one the oracle weights most heavily:
`metrics.Calculate`'s `TotalIncome`, `TotalExpenses` and `NetSavings` must
exclude every `Transfer` row. Check for yourself which other consumers read
income/outflow totals and whether they need the same treatment — a task in
this repo previously relabelled stored data correctly while every existing
consumer kept reading it the old way, silently (ruling 2026-08-16f).

**7. Pipeline position.** Classification runs after account stamping, StableID
assignment and dedup, and before income/outflow classification. See design §2.
Do not reorder any other stage.
