{{/* Glossary — hover-tooltips on headers cover most cases, but a visible disclosure helps mobile users and anyone who wants the full reference at a glance. */}}
What do these columns mean?
Strategy
Roth conversion plan: which marginal tax bracket to fill with conversions, and over which ages (e.g. Fill 24% bracket, 67→72).
SS (P/S)
Social Security claim ages — Primary / Spouse.
End Portfolio
Deterministic projection ending balance in today's dollars (uses your expected return — no randomness).
{{if gt $to.MonteCarloRuns 0}}
MC Median ▼ (ranking metric)
Median ending balance across {{$to.MonteCarloRuns}} Monte Carlo runs in today's dollars. Strategies are sorted by this column — it's the most robust single-number summary because randomized return paths capture sequence-of-returns risk.
{{end}}
Lifetime Tax
Total federal + state + NIIT taxes paid over the projection, in today's dollars. (IRMAA is tracked separately as a cost.)
Peak Rate
Highest per-year effective marginal rate this candidate reaches over the projection — a measured rate, not a bracket-table lookup, so capital-gain stacking and the Social Security taxability phase-in can push it above every statutory bracket. Reporting only; not part of the ranking metric. Shown as “—” when the projection never recorded a rate.
Roth Conv
Total dollars this candidate actually converted to Roth over the projection, in nominal (not inflation-adjusted) dollars — unlike the other dollar columns here. Reflects what the engine realized, not what was planned. Shown as “—” when the candidate never converted.
Δ vs Baseline
Change in deterministic End Portfolio vs your current saved scenario. Green = improvement, amber = regression.
{{if gt $to.MonteCarloRuns 0}}
MC Surv%
Percent of MC runs in which the portfolio lasted the full projection. Not the ranking metric — a strategy can have high survival but a lower median (or vice versa).
{{end}}
{{if gt $to.MonteCarloRuns 0}}

Why MC Median can disagree with End Portfolio: the deterministic projection assumes you hit your expected return every year. Monte Carlo runs your plan against {{$to.MonteCarloRuns}} randomized return paths — a strategy that does well on the smooth path can still rank lower once volatility and bad sequences are modeled.
